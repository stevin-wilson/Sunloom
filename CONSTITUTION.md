## Preamble

This product exists to give people attractive, appropriate, high-quality wallpapers without requiring them to search for images, understand display specifications, manage files, or continually adjust settings.

The product shall be:

- Free to obtain and use
- Lightweight and unobtrusive
- Respectful of privacy and system resources
- Visually reliable across different displays
- Strictly limited to safe-for-work images and categories
- Clear about where images come from
- Resilient when the internet or an image provider is unavailable
- Predictable enough to trust, but varied enough to remain enjoyable

Whenever convenience conflicts with content safety, user control, privacy, image rights, or device reliability, those protections take priority.

---

## Article I: Core Product Promise

### 1. Effortless beauty
The user should be able to install the application, choose a small number of preferences, and receive attractive wallpapers automatically.

### 2. Appropriate throughout the day
The application shall support visually lighter and darker wallpapers based on the user's local time and preferences. "Light" and "dark" shall describe the actual visual characteristics of the image, not merely a category assigned by an image provider.

### 3. Native-looking results
A wallpaper should look intentionally selected for the display on which it appears. It should not look like an image that was arbitrarily stretched, enlarged, or cut apart.

---

## Article II: User Sovereignty

1. **The user remains in control:** Immediate manual change, previous wallpaper, pause/resume, keep, reject forever, per-display policy (where the platform permits; see Article IV §1 and Article XVIII §1), lock/workspace policy, source/category toggles. No NSFW override permitted. **[v0.4]**
2. **No irreversible actions:** Preserve restoration capability for the baseline wallpaper (including resetting to the system default wallpaper and optional user-selected restore file) and never leave a broken wallpaper on uninstall/disable. **[v0.4]**
3. **No attention traps:** No ads, sponsored content, streaks, review nagging, promotional notifications, mandatory accounts, or unrelated feeds.

---

## Article III: Time and Appearance

1. **Time policy:** Fixed clock times, Sunrise and sunset, Follow OS light/dark appearance, or Manual only (no automatic time-based switching).
2. **Location privacy:** Sunrise and sunset computed locally using user-supplied city/region (with full IANA timezone / DST rules) or coordinates without continuous location tracking or GPS permissions. **[v0.4]**
3. **Graceful transitions:** Avoid rapid catch-up changes after sleep/wake; if multiple scheduled transitions elapsed while asleep, apply only the current target state once. **[v0.4]**

---

## Article IV: Display Awareness and Image Quality

1. **Each display is inspected honestly:** Resolution, aspect ratio, orientation, physical PPI, scaling, crop, and quality requirements are measured from real display metrics (`Display.Mode` physical pixels and calibrated EDID PPI, falling back only to `WindowMetrics` when `DisplayManager` returns none, and refusing to apply rather than inventing fallback dimensions when display bounds are unknown). Where the underlying OS shares a single system wallpaper surface across connected monitors, the app states this plainly, defaults to `SAME_ON_ALL_DISPLAYS`, and renders a single envelope bitmap covering the aspect range of **currently connected** displays (`e = r_src.coerceIn(r_min, r_max)` bounded by output pixel caps) so each display's center-crop retains the focal subject and passes §2; displays that cannot be satisfied are excluded and disclosed without degrading others, and single-display sharpest crop (`OPTIMIZE_PRIMARY_SHARPEST`) remains available if a launcher scrolls wider-than-screen bitmaps. Disconnected display history is diagnostic-only and never constrains active crops. **[v0.6]**
2. **Native-resolution quality (tiered physical-PPI floor):** Never invent source dimensions; always measure the real decoded image bounds. Effective PPI is derived from `DisplayMetrics.xdpi/ydpi` within `60..600` (treating uncalibrated VESA `72`/`96` placeholder pairs inconsistent with physical resolution as untrusted and falling back to `densityDpi`). Measured on post-crop pixels per target display: **[v0.6]**
   - `< 120 ppi`: `>= 0.98` pixel coverage (`98%`: an enlargement of at most 2%, which cannot be seen, so a source whose largest rendition is a few pixels short, such as a 3400 px print on a 3440 px display, is not turned away) **[v0.15]**
   - `120–209 ppi`: `>= 0.80` pixel coverage (`80%`)
   - `>= 210 ppi`: `>= 0.65` pixel coverage (`65%`) **and** post-crop long edge `>= 1600 px`
   - Blurred Frame Fill remains opt-in down to `0.50` coverage (`50%`).
3. **Quality is more than resolution:** Consider aspect ratio, sharpness, crop survivability, and legibility. Brightness is judged on the perceptual lightness (CIE L\*) of the median pixel of the cropped picture, not on a linear mean: `DARK` needs a median `L* <= 40`, white desktop labels with a WCAG contrast of at least `4.5:1` over the busiest 10% of the icon column, top bar and shelf (`3:1` over the clock zone) and glare `<= 6%`; `LIGHT` needs a median `L* >= 52` with moderate contrast spread; everything else is `NEUTRAL`. The picture is reduced to the coarse grid this test reads by averaging every pixel of each cell, so a detailed photograph is classed the same whatever size it was decoded at **[v0.15]**. Each image also records median lightness, label contrast, warmth and dominant hue for variety. **[v0.14]**
4. **Crop before stretch:** Stretching or nonuniform scaling is strictly prohibited. When blurred fill is used with several displays, the whole picture stays visible inside every display's centre-crop of the shared canvas. **[v0.18]**
5. **Subject-aware cropping:** Deterministic, testable focal-point cropping that avoids cutting through primary subjects or placing clutter under clock/UI zones, persisted and applied when preparing the final bitmap. **[v0.4]** It also prefers rule-of-thirds placement, keeps the subject out from under the icon column and shelf, measures brightness and contrast on the picture inside a neutral mat or frame that appears on both opposite sides, and shows the whole picture (blurred fill, only when allowed) or skips it (`COMPOSITION_CROPS_TOO_MUCH`) when a display-shaped crop would keep less than half of its area. **[v0.14]**
6. **Display changes:** Display-set changes are re-checked and reconciled safely on app foreground, on debounced `DisplayListener` events, and at the start of every scheduled worker run. A display set is identified by what its displays are (size, density, quality tier), not by Android's display ids, and when every display is asleep they all stay in the set, so re-plugging a monitor or a screen timeout does not re-plan or re-apply the wallpaper; with auto-rotate on, the built-in display keeps its natural-orientation identity while it turns. **[v0.6, v0.17]**

## Article V: Lock and Workspace Screens

1. **Separate destinations:** Same image on both (`Home & Lock screen`), Workspace only (`Home screen only`), or Lock screen only (`Lock screen only`). **[v0.5]**
2. **Capability honesty:** Detect platform capabilities rather than assuming them, verify `WallpaperManager.getWallpaperId` changes after apply, clearly explain unavailable features, and never pretend a wallpaper was applied when it was not. **[v0.4]**
3. **Legibility zones:** Account for clock/date, desktop icons, and taskbar/shelf zones.

## Article VI: Image Sources, Rights, and Attribution

1. **No unauthorized scraping:** Use only authorized public APIs, open-content licenses (`CC0`, `Public Domain`, `CC BY`, `CC BY-SA`), or direct user-provided files. "Authorized" means a documented, publicly offered API whose terms permit programmatic retrieval by end-user devices. An endpoint is unauthorized if it is undocumented, internal, reverse-engineered, or intended only for the provider's own products. This holds even when the endpoint is reachable without credentials. **[v0.3]**
2. **Preserve provenance:** Retain source, asset ID, original page, creator name/page (only when genuinely provided by the source), license, date retrieved, attribution, real original & rendition dimensions, safety classification, perceptual hash (`dHash`), and SHA-256 checksum. Expose via "About this wallpaper". Also retain the license URL and version, the adapter version, the safety gate version, and the per-layer gate outcome (Article VII §5). **[v0.4]**
3. **Replaceable source modules:** Every source is an isolated adapter registered in `SourceRegistry` that can be disabled cleanly. **[v0.4]**
4. **Source admission:** No adapter ships until the source is entered in the Source Register (Schedule A) and meets all of the following: **[v0.3]**
   - a. **Documented terms:** The source offers a documented API, and its terms have been reviewed. The register records the review date and reviewer.
   - b. **Credential-free:** The adapter works with no API key, token, or OAuth client credentials. This includes "public demo" keys. Any credential shipped inside the app is an embedded reusable secret.
   - c. **Per-asset license:** Each asset carries a machine-readable license or source-verified public-domain status per Schedule A. A blanket assumption for uncurated uploads is not sufficient.
   - d. **Attribution data:** Machine-readable creator and attribution fields are sufficient to satisfy §2 and §6.
   - e. **Stable identity:** Assets have stable identifiers and a canonical public page URL.
   - f. **Transport:** Metadata and image hosts are HTTPS-only.
   - g. **Rate headroom:** Normal scheduled use by a single device stays at or below 50% of the source's published anonymous limits.
   - h. **Safety profile:** The register documents which safety signals the source provides (flags, categories, curation) and its known risk profile.
5. **Per-asset license verification:** An asset is eligible only if its license resolves to one of these: **[v0.3]**
   - CC0 1.0
   - Public Domain Mark 1.0, or an explicit public-domain verification rule defined for the source in Schedule A (such as museum `is_public_domain` flags or US Government NASA public-domain rules)
   - CC BY 2.0–4.0
   - CC BY-SA 2.0–4.0

   Everything else is rejected. This includes NC and ND variants, CC 1.0 licenses, GFDL-only, provider-specific licenses (such as the Unsplash License), "free to use" wording without a license identifier, non-empty Wikimedia `Restrictions` (personality/trademark/insignia restrictions), and missing licenses. Multi-licensed works (e.g., `CC BY-SA 3.0` + `GFDL`) are eligible when at least one offered license is in the allowlist above, and that allowlisted license is recorded at retrieval time. ND is excluded because cropping may constitute an adaptation. **[v0.4]**
6. **Attribution display:** For CC BY and CC BY-SA assets, "About this wallpaper" shows the title, creator, source, license name with a link, and a note that the image was cropped and resized. It must be reachable within one interaction from the main surface and must work offline. Public-domain assets still show creator and source where known. Procedural seeds state they were generated on-device under CC0 without fabricated URLs. **Export:** when a wallpaper is saved with "Save as…", the credit travels with the file. JPEG exports embed title, creator, source, license and the modification note as metadata; CC BY and CC BY-SA exports additionally require a companion attribution file, because embedded metadata is easily stripped. Only already-approved renditions can be exported; export never re-fetches or bypasses the safety gate. **[v0.4, v0.9]**
7. **Network conduct:** Adapters shall: **[v0.3]**
   - send an identifying User-Agent of the form `Sunloom/<version> (+<project page URL>)` (the project's GitHub page; no mailbox is needed) **[v0.36]**;
   - honor `429`, `Retry-After`, and persisted per-source exponential backoff;
   - use conditional requests (`ETag` / `If-Modified-Since`) where supported and applicable;
   - enforce a per-source request budget inside the adapter;
   - space sequential requests without bursts and never burst on wake, unlock, or network reconnection;
   - fetch images only from hosts documented in Schedule A, validating every HTTP redirect hop (max 3 hops) against the host allowlist. **[v0.4]**
8. **Adapter failure handling:** If a response does not match the expected schema (for example, a missing license, missing ID, or missing/"Unknown author" creator on an attribution-required `CC BY` / `CC BY-SA` asset), the affected asset is rejected. For verified Public Domain / CC0 assets where an individual human creator does not exist or is unknown (such as automated orbital spacecraft imagery or anonymous historical works), institutional or archival attribution is permitted. After repeated consecutive failures, the adapter marks itself degraded or auto-disabled in source diagnostics. Disabling an adapter for reliability reasons does not purge assets it already approved. **[v0.4]**
9. **Source removal and purge:** When a source is removed, or its rights or safety status is downgraded in `SourceRegistry`, its assets are purged from the reserve and cache on startup and on the next worker run. Any wallpaper currently showing from that source is replaced from another approved source, or else the baseline is restored. The purge must never leave a broken wallpaper. **[v0.4]**
10. **User-provided files:** Imported files are copied into quarantine as raw bytes, verified through L3/L4 (respecting EXIF orientation and real dimensions; L1 and L2 metadata screening are not applicable to user-selected local files), and stored in the local library retaining their original approved image bytes (`JPEG`, `PNG`, or `WEBP` without PNG re-encoding) with provenance marked `"User-provided"` and no fabricated URL. L5 ("Reject forever" blocklist) applies normally. Images may arrive from the Photo Picker / SAF, drag-and-drop, or the system Share sheet and Open with (`SEND`, `SEND_MULTIPLE`, `VIEW`; `content://` only, at most 20 items per intent). The declared MIME type only routes the intent; L3 verifies the real bytes. **[v0.6, v0.9]**

## Article VII: Content Safety and Personal Taste

1. **Strict safe-content policy:** Mandatory, non-configurable NSFW exclusion across queries, categories, thumbnails, previews, cache, and wallpapers. Fail closed when safety classification is missing, uncertain, or conflicting. Safety classification must never be derived merely from license validity. **[v0.4]**
2. **Safe defaults:** Exclude violence, hate symbols, disturbing medical content, political campaigning, and misleading system UI prompts. Also exclude by default: **[v0.4]**
   - photographs or artworks whose primary subject is an identifiable real person or portrait;
   - images dominated by text;
   - montages, collages, N×M arrays and multi-panel figure plates rather than one coherent scene **[v0.13]**;
   - weapons as the primary subject;
   - animals in distress;
   - logos or insignia as the primary subject.
   Exclusion of identifiable real persons relies on tiered L2 metadata/classification screening (and optional reject-only on-device face detection at L4). **[v0.4]**
3. **Preference without surveillance:** Local-first personalization and repetition control. Kept and disliked counts on the device nudge category and source weights within `0.6..1.6`; nothing is excluded and nothing leaves the device. **[v0.14]**
4. **Layered safety gate:** Every asset passes every layer, in order. Any layer may reject. No single layer may approve on its own. **[v0.4]**
   - **L1 — Eligibility:** The asset comes from an admitted, enabled adapter in `SourceRegistry`, and its license and creator attribution pass Article VI §5 and §8.
   - **L2 — Metadata screen:** The source's own safety flags must indicate safe: an online adapter may assign `SafetyClassification.SAFE` only by citing an active Schedule A safety signal (curation membership, classification/department allowlist hit, or verified public-domain + visual medium allowlist), recorded in `safetyClassificationSource`. Adapters must never inject synthetic safe tags into `rawCategoriesAndTags`. Metadata is Unicode NFKD normalized, lowercased in `Locale.ROOT`, diacritic-stripped, and plural/stem-normalized before matching against `SafetyDenylist` (`DENYLIST_VERSION`): **[v0.6]**
     - **Tier 1 (Unconditional Denylist — matched across title, description, tags, and attribution):** NSFW, nudity (including classical-nude terms such as `odalisque`, `bather`), graphic violence/gore, hate/extremism, political campaigning, and system UI spoofing, plus explicit portrait phrases in prose (`"portrait of"`, `"self portrait"`, `"headshot"`, `"mugshot"`, `"press conference"`).
     - **Tier 2 (Subject / Tag / Classification Denylist — matched across title, category/subject tags, and museum classification/department, avoiding false rejections on descriptive prose like *"An astronaut aboard the ISS..."* or *"North face"*):** single-token person/portrait terms (`portrait`, `portraits`, `person`, `people`, `man`, `men`, `woman`, `women`, `girl`, `boy`, `child`, `children`, `crowd`, `face`, `bust`, `figurative`), weapons (`weapon`, `gun`, `rifle`, `pistol`, `sword`, `knife`, `bomb`, `armor`, `firearm`), insignia (`logo`, `emblem`, `insignia`, `mission patch`, `coat of arms`), and medical/distress terms (`autopsy`, `cadaver`, `skull`, `anatomy`, `disease`).
     - **L2c (Composite layout, v0.13):** `montage`, `photomontage` and `collage` in the title or a source category, a grid category such as `3x2 arrays` (bare `array`, `composite`, `triptych` and `mosaic` are not matched), or a description that opens with a figure caption (`Figure 2`, `Fig. 3`) or carries `(A)` … `(B)` panel labels, reject the asset (`COMPOSITE_OR_MULTI_PANEL_LAYOUT`). Prose that merely mentions a montage does not reject. **[v0.13]**
     - **L2d (Not a scene, NASA only, v0.25):** `map`, `chart`, `graph`, `diagram`, `illustration`, `animation`, `visualization`, `simulation`, `rendering` or an artist's concept in the title, a source category or the first sentence of the description is rejected as `NON_PHOTOGRAPHIC_CONTENT`. Tier 1 also carries the press-event phrases `media day`, `media interview`, `media briefing`, `science briefing`, and `news conference`, which agency photo sites attach to scenery-like keywords. L2d applies to NASA records only, and also rejects self-descriptions such as `this illustration`, `this map` and `computer modeling` anywhere in the description. **[v0.26]** For NASA the same layer also applies the record-family rules of Schedule A (`NASA_RECORD_NOT_ADMITTED`), so stored records are re-vetted by the gate version.
     - **L2b (Safe Visual Domain):** Title and source-supplied tags/classifications must match at least one recognized safe domain token (`SafetyDenylist.SAFE_DOMAIN_KEYWORDS`, excluding generic words like `"featured"`). Museum adapters that already restrict results by department/classification (`met_museum_cc0`, `cma_cc0`, `smk_open`, `artic_cc0`) may rely on the category their query declared when their tags carry no keyword; tags must still be present and L2a/L2c still apply. A missing keyword or missing tags is a retryable, display-scoped outcome (never a permanent blocklist entry) and is re-evaluated whenever `DENYLIST_VERSION` changes. **[v0.14]**
     - **Benign phrases (v0.14):** `naked eye`, `bikini atoll`, `blood moon`, `blood orange` and `blood red sky` are removed before the Tier 1 scan; every other use of those words is still matched.
     - **Blocklist check:** Asset ID, SHA-256 (when known), and perceptual hash (`dHash`) are checked against the persistent blocklist.
   - **L3 — Payload & structural verification:** Before full bitmap decoding, untrusted payload bytes in quarantine are verified for magic-byte format signature (`JPEG`, `PNG`, `WEBP`), byte-size bounds (`4 KB..40 MB`), `BitmapFactory.Options.inJustDecodeBounds` header dimensions (`<= 16,384 px` per side and `<= 100 MP` total), MIME-type agreement with magic bytes, and SHA-256 blocklist verification.
   - **L4 — Rendition check:** After downsampled or full decoding (catching `OutOfMemoryError` and exceptions as rejections) and subject-aware cropping (`ImageEvaluator`), the rendition is verified using **real decoded dimensions** for native pixel coverage (Article IV §2), aspect-ratio integrity, legibility-zone glare/clutter bounds, and 64-bit perceptual difference hash (`dHash`) verification against the persistent blocklist.
   - **L5 — Post-approval feedback:** "Reject forever" persists the asset ID, SHA-256, and 64-bit `dHash` into the SQLite `blocklist` table so near-duplicates are rejected across restarts and across all sources.
5. **Gate versioning:** The composite gate version combines `SafetyGate.GATE_VERSION`, `SafetyDenylist.DENYLIST_VERSION`, evaluator version/thresholds, and blocklist schema version, and is stored per asset in SQLite alongside a structured `GateTrace` of actual per-layer outcomes (`gateLayerOutcomes`). If the active wallpaper's gate version is outdated on startup, or when any reserve asset is selected for display or inspected during a background refresh, L1–L4 are re-evaluated using stored metadata and local image bytes, and failures are purged (replacing any invalidated active wallpaper via platform-verified apply or restoring baseline). Without a known display the brightness and crop evaluation cannot be redone, so the record keeps its old gate version until one is known. **[v0.6, v0.16]**
6. **Concrete fail-closed rules:** Any gate failure, unknown DB enum value, corrupt header, `OutOfMemoryError`, timeout, or production `SqliteLibraryStore` open failure is treated as a fail-closed state (never defaulting to `SAFE`, `CC0`, or a transient in-memory store that loses the blocklist across restarts). If SQLite cannot open, background automation is suspended and the UI surfaces a diagnostic error. A light store-watch job checks every 6 hours whether the store opens again and resumes automation by itself; it touches no data while the store is down, and opening the app or any normal re-arm removes it **[v0.24]**. If no approved assets remain, the current approved wallpaper stays in place or the baseline is restored. **[v0.6]**
7. **Quarantine:** Unapproved bytes live only in memory or `noBackupFilesDir/quarantine`, are deleted immediately upon gate completion, and any file older than 24 hours is swept on startup. **[v0.4]**
8. **Safe browsing surfaces:** Preview, history, and browse screens show only approved assets.
9. **Measurable acceptance:** No release ships unless the gate passes Schedule B.
10. **Safety data delivery:** Thresholds and denylists ship only inside signed app releases.

## Article VIII: Privacy and Data Minimization **[v0.4]**

1. **Local-first storage:** All user preferences, wallpaper history, schedules, provenance records, and blocklists are stored locally on the device.
2. **Zero telemetry:** The app includes no analytics SDKs, crash-reporting trackers, advertising identifiers, or developer-operated tracking endpoints. A crash is written to a small file in the app's private storage (kept out of Auto Backup, the latest five kept) and goes nowhere on its own; Storage & About can copy diagnostics (build, device, displays, source health, recent crashes; never location or photo titles) and open a pre-filled GitHub issue for the person to read and send. Release builds strip verbose, debug and info logging **[v0.35]**. The text it copies is scrubbed of file paths and content or file addresses, keeping only the host of a web address **[v0.44]**.
3. **Zero embedded secrets:** No API keys, OAuth client secrets, or keystore passwords may be embedded in source code, resources, or build scripts across any variant.

## Article IX: Offline Reliability and Reserve Management **[v0.4]**

1. **First-boot offline readiness:** On first launch (including in airplane mode), Sunloom synthesizes at least 3 Light and 3 Dark procedural CC0 wallpapers at the connected display's native resolution so the app functions without network access. Categories adopted from v0.8 onward must also ship with at least 2 Light and 2 Dark bundled seeds, so a user who enables only such a category still has an eligible offline fallback (the matcher excludes seeds from other categories). Cache eviction keeps one Light and one Dark seed per category. Rendered seeds are kept on disk (in the cache, which the system may reclaim) for the last two display sizes, so docking and undocking a monitor does not render them all again **[v0.43]**. The kept renders are filed under the version of the app and renderer that made them, so a release that changes a seed never shows the last release's pictures **[v0.44]**. Architecture & Skylines (1 Light, 0 Dark), Space & Astronomy (0 Light, 1 Dark), Scenic Fine Art and Abstract & Minimal predate this rule and are an open gap. **[v0.4, v0.11]**
2. **Protected reserve floor:** The local library maintains at least **3 Light + 3 Dark** approved wallpapers protected from cache eviction (in addition to active wallpapers, user favorites, and the baseline restore wallpaper).
3. **Atomic wallpaper changes:** A candidate wallpaper is fully downloaded, validated through L1–L4, and verified to exist on disk (`fileSizeBytes > 0`) before `WallpaperManager` is invoked. If applying a wallpaper fails, the scheduler advances to the next eligible reserve wallpaper rather than getting stuck.
4. **Event-driven schedule recovery:** Time changes (`TIME_SET`) and timezone changes (`TIMEZONE_CHANGED`) reschedule the next transition immediately.
5. **Restored libraries heal:** Auto Backup restores the library database but not the image files. At startup and at the start of scheduled work, rows whose image file is missing are marked evicted (provenance retained), counts exclude them, and the next sync downloads them again, keeping their Kept flag and play history. A personal photo or the baseline copy that the restore could not bring back cannot be downloaded again: its row is kept but marked gone the same way, so its missing size no longer counts toward the cache, evicts real images or holds back top-ups, and the person is told once how many personal photos were lost. **[v0.14, v0.30]**

## Article X: Resource Restraint and Network Budgets **[v0.4]**

1. **Separated scheduling workers:** Wallpaper switching (`ChangeWorker`, scheduled via one-time delay to the next solar/clock transition with no network requirement; Follow-OS mode, which has no such transition, polls every 15 minutes and leaves a wallpaper that already suits the theme alone **[v0.19]**; a poll that finds the theme and the wallpaper exactly as it last verified them ends without reading the library or probing displays, a system default the person chose is left alone until the theme changes, Follow-OS sets no backstop alarm, and while the app is running a change of theme is acted on at once **[v0.42]**) is separated from catalog replenishment (`RefreshWorker`, periodic every 3 hours, independent of the change schedule so manual-mode users keep a fresh reserve; paused automation stops both) **[v0.14]**.
2. **Battery and network restraint:** Background catalog downloads pause when Battery Saver is active (`deferOnBatterySaver`), and, because `wifiOnlyDownloads` ("unmetered only") is on by default, whenever the active network is metered. "Metered" is what the network itself reports (`NET_CAPABILITY_NOT_METERED`), not the radio in use, so a phone hotspot counts as metered. A person who allows metered networks still gets none while Android's Data Saver is on. The app downloads at most 300 MB in the background per local day; a run that has used it makes no request, and the bytes of every download count, including pictures then rejected **[v0.41]**. Data Saver restricts only when it is on and this app is not exempt from it; page and picture bytes are counted as bytes, from the start of each sync **[v0.44]**. Offline wallpaper switches still run on schedule. A background refresh also waits until the device is not low on storage, checks the network and battery rules before it does any other work, and makes no request at all when the reserve needs no top-up; an explicit tap on Refresh is the person's own request and is not held back by these background rules **[v0.40]**.
3. **Bounded disk cache:** Default cache ceiling is 1 GB (configurable by the user; installs still on the former 250 MB default are raised once). User-provided pictures are never evicted, because they cannot be fetched again **[v0.19]**. When evicting old image files to stay under budget, already-shown images go first (least recently applied first) and never-shown images last, and provenance rows are retained or cleanly marked evicted so the matcher never selects a missing file. The limit counts the bytes the image files really take on disk, not the sizes rows recorded. Once a day, on opening the app and in the refresh worker, files in the image folder that no row refers to are removed after a one-hour grace period (never while the library has no rows), and rows that only record a picture that was evicted, never Kept, not shown on screen and neither fetched nor shown for 90 days are dropped; the blocklist and the rejection log are never touched **[v0.33]**. A download is held in memory once, not twice (one buffer of the announced size, handed over without a copy), is released as soon as the picture has been sampled, and is torn down at once when the sync is cancelled, so a stalled network read cannot hold a sync, or its memory, until a timeout **[v0.38]**.
4. **Per-source request budgets & sync rotation:** Each online adapter enforces its Schedule A per-source HTTP request cap (`5–10` HTTP requests per sync run across metadata and image downloads, bounded by an outer `BudgetedSourceHttpClient` ceiling of `<= 10`), spaces sequential requests by at least 150–250 ms, and honors `HTTP 429` / `Retry-After` (both delta-seconds and HTTP-date) with persisted backoff. Each catalog replenishment cycle polls at most 3 online sources via deficit-aware round-robin rotation (prioritizing dark-capable photographic sources when evening reserves are low). **[v0.14]** The reserve aims for 12 unseen fetched images for each of Light and Dark (a NEUTRAL image counts for the mode it fits, Article XI §1) and tops up in rounds (up to 4 in the app, 3 in the background worker) while a round adds images and the target is not met; fetching pauses when the cache is 90% full or 100 unseen fetched images are already waiting (Dark images can be rare, so one short mode must not fill the cache), automatic top-ups are spaced 10 minutes apart after one that added images and 60 minutes after one that found nothing, and the Refresh button always fetches. The Met `/v1/search` endpoint was retired on 2026-10-01 and the adapter uses the paginated `/v1.1/search`; its cursor finishes each 12-id search window before moving to the next query and window; NASA is queried with a wider page because many records carry no keywords and are rejected by L2b. Each source keeps its own persisted fetch cursor, which starts at a random point on first use and advances only after that source answers, so a dead network never skips unseen content and installs do not all crawl the same pages. (The automatic top-ups made while the app is open follow the same network rule: they run on an unmetered network and not over a metered one. A tap on Refresh is the person's own request and always runs, but on a metered network the first tap only warns that it may use mobile data, and the next one goes ahead.) **[v0.41]** A source whose requests all fail counts as failed, and Diagnostics shows what the last sync fetched, added, skipped and failed per source, what each source (and each fallback such as the offline seeds and imported images) currently holds in the reserve by brightness, and the skipped candidates grouped by source and reason **[v0.15]**. Candidates that advertise a size too small for the display are skipped before downloading, a picture almost identical (informative perceptual hash within 6 bits) to one already in the library is rejected as `NEAR_DUPLICATE_OF_LIBRARY`, and at most three never-shown images per creator are kept (institutional NASA imagery is exempt). **[v0.6]**

## Article XI: Matching, Personalization, and Explainability **[v0.5]**

1. **Brightness fidelity:** Daytime periods select `LIGHT` (falling back to a `NEUTRAL` image whose median `L* >= 46`); night periods select `DARK` (falling back to a `NEUTRAL` image whose median `L* <= 46`). A `NEUTRAL` image on the wrong side of that split is a last resort, used only after repeating a recent exact match and before crossing to the opposite brightness; an image whose lightness has not yet been measured fits both. **[v0.15]**
2. **Repetition, source-diversity & non-seed priority control:** Online and user-provided sources take priority over the bundled procedural `seed_reserve` (which serves as an offline fallback when no eligible non-seed wallpaper is available, or when the best one does not suit the target brightness and a seed does) **[v0.19]**. Recently applied wallpapers and the currently active wallpaper are deprioritized in favor of unseen (`appliedCount == 0`) or least-recently applied wallpapers, interleaving sources by deprioritizing the active wallpaper's `sourceId` on ties, with a gentle preference for Kept (`isFavorite`) items. **[v0.5]** **[v0.14]** Selection is varied, not a fixed cycle. Within each brightness step the order is: an unused exact-brightness image, then an unused `NEUTRAL` image that fits the target (so a small pool never ping-pongs between the same two or three), then a recently used exact or fitting `NEUTRAL` image, then the current wallpaper if it fits, then a `NEUTRAL` image that does not fit (Article XI §1), and only then the opposite brightness **[v0.15]**. "Recent" covers the active and previous wallpapers plus the most recently applied images (up to eight, at most half of the eligible pool). Among candidates, never-shown images come first, otherwise the least recently applied half; a different source than the active one is required when one exists; the pick is random, weighted up for Kept images and for the local taste profile (Article VII §3), and weighted down for images resembling the last ones shown (same category, same dominant hue, or a near-identical perceptual hash). Previews of the next wallpaper use a deterministic variant so they do not change on every rebuild.
3. **Transient vs. permanent rejections:** Safety, license, and user rejections permanently blocklist the asset ID, SHA-256, and `dHash`. Resolution/quality rejections are scoped to the display dimensions measured and do not permanently ban the asset if display geometry changes.
4. **Decision explainability:** Every approved or rejected candidate records a human-readable explanation of its gate and evaluator metrics visible in the UI.

## Article XII: Onboarding and User Interface Conduct **[v0.5]**

1. **Transparent setup & Laws of UX:** First-run onboarding previews the initial Light and Dark reserve and lets the user choose schedule mode, categories, and destination policy using progressive disclosure before enabling background automation. Setup is four short steps with a visible "Step N of 4": what Sunloom does (in plain words: where the pictures come from, that downloads wait for an unmetered network, that nothing leaves the device), when it changes, what it shows (with the reason NASA is off by default), and a preview. The first step can finish everything with the defaults. The city for sunrise and sunset is guessed from the device's time zone (never from the location) and can be changed in one click; a reopened setup starts from the person's own choices **[v0.46]**. Dry-run mode is internal-only and not user-facing. Nothing chosen in setup is permanent: Schedule has an "Automatic switching" switch, so someone who turned it off can turn it on again, and "Setup Guide" shows the first-run screen again with their current choices, saving nothing until they finish it **[v0.29]**. The first thing in Storage & About is "About Sunloom": the version and build, the licence, the privacy policy, the source, and the open-source licences Sunloom is built from (a curated list, with a test that fails when a runtime dependency is missing from it) **[v0.45]**. Every status message shown to the user comes from string resources, so none is fixed in code and all can be translated **[v0.16]**. A message too long for its line opens in full when tapped (and says whether it is open); a failed sync, or one that reached no source, offers Try again; while photos are being checked a progress bar and "photo N of M" show; Add Photo puts the photo in the reserve and offers Apply now rather than changing the wallpaper behind the person's back (a single dropped or shared photo still applies, since that was the request); and the line under the message describes the schedule in use (sunrise and sunset, the system theme, the chosen hours, or "when you tap Next") **[v0.47]**.
2. **Responsive main thread:** All SQLite queries, bitmap decoding, seed rendering, and `WallpaperManager` calls execute off the main thread (`Dispatchers.IO` / `Dispatchers.Default`), and previews decode downsampled thumbnails matching the computed display crop. When a wallpaper is applied, the picture is decoded no smaller than the part of it that will be shown (so a display is never fed an upscaled crop) and no larger than that needs, and it is cropped and scaled in one pass so the unscaled crop is never held alongside the source and the result **[v0.37]**. A display-change notice for the display set the library was already reconciled for (a resize, a focus change, a start) costs no pass and writes nothing; checking whether a shared picture is an image asks another process and so happens off the main thread; and the remembered-displays file is rewritten only when a display is new, changed, or its "last seen" time has gone stale (ten minutes) **[v0.43]**. When the system asks the app to give memory back, hiding the window frees the preview cache, and real memory pressure or moving to the background frees every in-memory cache (previews, rendered seeds, saved HTTP responses); all rebuild on demand **[v0.39]**.
3. **Safe keyboard navigation:** Destructive actions (`Reject Forever`, reserve removal) act immediately and offer a timed Undo action (the exclusion or file deletion is finalized when the Undo window ends, a new destructive action starts, or the app stops); irreversible resets (`Reset to System Default`, `Reset Sunloom`) require confirmation. `Reset Sunloom`, in Storage & About, erases everything the app keeps (library, imported photos, Kept and blocked pictures, settings, images and staging files), puts the device's original wallpaper back, stops all scheduled work, and returns the app to first-run setup; its confirmation names what will go **[v0.34]**. Reset waits, for a bounded time, for any background attempt already running to stop before it erases **[v0.44]**. Removing several reserve items at once (multi-select) is one undoable action, and `Ctrl+Z` triggers the pending Undo. **[v0.7, v0.9]**
4. **Googlebook desktop conduct:** The workspace is edge-to-edge with safe-drawing insets, declares a default and minimum free-form window size, supports keyboard (Ctrl+1–5 tab switching alongside single-key accelerators, focus returned to the workspace after dialogs), mouse and trackpad (hover states, context menus, double-click activation, targets of at least 48dp), drag-and-drop image import, announces status changes to assistive technology via live regions, gives every operable control (including switches) an accessible name, and exposes each section title as a heading. Every interactive control shows a hand cursor and a visible focus ring. `Esc` backs out of the current context (menu, search text, selection, then focus returns to the workspace). System Back does the same one level up: from any section other than Home it returns to Home, and only on Home does it leave the app; the app opts in to predictive back. `Ctrl+Z` undoes, `Ctrl+C` copies the attribution, `Ctrl+S` saves the wallpaper, and `Ctrl+,` opens Schedule. The Reserve offers list and grid layouts, `Ctrl`/`Shift` multi-select, and arrow/Home/End/Page navigation. The Today preview zooms with `Ctrl`+wheel or pinch and resets on double click. Fullscreen requests true window fullscreen where the OS provides it (API 35+) and hides system bars otherwise. A busy pointer shows over non-interactive areas during long operations. Pointer targets equal their visual bounds. **[v0.7, v0.9]**

## Article XVIII: Platform and Distribution **[v0.4]**

1. **Target:** Googlebook OS (`arm64-v8a` Snapdragon and `x86_64` Intel), distributed as a signed APK on GitHub Releases (`github` flavor) and, later, through Google Play (`play` flavor); both flavors share one applicationId and signing key, and only the `github` flavor may contain update-check code, limited to the hosts in `UpdateHosts` **[v0.28]**; the update check runs only when the person taps "Check for updates" in About, asks only `api.github.com`, accepts only a published release, and offers a link to its page: it never downloads or installs anything **[v0.45]**, and the person may opt in, once, in setup, to a check made when the app is opened at most once a week (never in the background, which would need a notification permission); it is off unless chosen **[v0.46]**. The Platform adapter uses only public, documented Android platform APIs (`WallpaperManager`). Because standard Android shares one system wallpaper surface across connected displays, `SAME_ON_ALL_DISPLAYS` is the default display policy (rendering a single multi-display envelope bitmap across currently connected screens per Article IV §1, with `OPTIMIZE_PRIMARY_SHARPEST` available as fallback) and `INDEPENDENT_PER_DISPLAY` is disclosed as unsupported by the OS. Support for separate lock-screen and workspace destinations is detected and verified per flag (`FLAG_SYSTEM`, `FLAG_LOCK`) via `WallpaperManager.getWallpaperId` after apply, per Article V §2. **[v0.6]**
2. **Minimal permissions:** The app requests only `INTERNET` and `SET_WALLPAPER` directly, plus the normal install-time permissions merged by AndroidX WorkManager (`ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`, `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED`, `WAKE_LOCK`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`). All runtime/dangerous permissions (location, media/storage, camera, contacts, notifications) are strictly forbidden. User files are imported through the system Photo Picker / SAF, drag-and-drop, or the Share sheet / Open with, and exported through the system file picker (`CreateDocument`); none of these need a permission. **[v0.4, v0.9]**
3. **No secrets in the package:** Distributed packages can be decompiled. Article VI §4(b) and Zero Embedded Secrets apply to every build variant, including debug and test builds.
4. **Store declarations:**
   - Applies to the `play` flavor: the Play Data safety declaration must accurately reflect that the developer collects no user data. **[v0.28]**
   - The privacy policy must name every source host the app contacts and state that those hosts receive the device's network requests.
   - Auto Backup rules (`dataExtractionRules` / `fullBackupContent`) back up user preferences and the SQLite database file (checkpointed when the app stops and at the end of every background run, so the excluded `-wal` file holds nothing a backup would miss) (a background run that wrote nothing checkpoints nothing and opens nothing); what the app works out about this device and moment, such as what a Follow-OS poll verified, is kept in a file the backup does not include **[v0.44]** while excluding `-wal`/`-shm` transient files and the `wallpapers/` and `quarantine/` image directories. **[v0.6]**
   - The content rating questionnaire is answered conservatively.
5. **Lightweight verification & optional on-device AI:** Core safety and visual evaluation ship as deterministic, zero-bloat verification (`SafetyGate` + `ImageEvaluator`). System-provided or bundled on-device AI features may be added only as an extra reject-only signal. They must never be a sole approver or compromise laptop resource restraint (Preamble & Article IV §5).
6. **Window model:** Sunloom runs as a single window (`launchMode="singleTask"`): relaunching focuses the existing window and any images it carries are imported into it. Wallpaper state is global to the device, so parallel windows would only contend for the same database and schedule. **[v0.9]**

## Schedule B: Safety Gate Acceptance Tests **[v0.4]**

1. **Evaluation corpus:** Version-controlled test fixtures in `SafetyGateTest` and `SourceAdaptersTest` (and `app/src/test/resources/safety-corpus/` where external fixtures are used) covering three mandatory sets (minimum 25 cases total):
   - a **known-unsafe set** (explicit NSFW, nudity, plural forms like `"Female Nudes"`, graphic violence, hate symbols);
   - a **known-safe set** (daylight mountain landscapes including *"North Face of the Eiger"* and *"Death Valley"*, ocean horizons, architectural exteriors, ISS astronaut-captured orbital Earth photography, astronomical nebulae including *"supernova explosion"*, and valid nature phrases like *"adult bald eagle"*);
   - a **hard-negative set** (classical art nudity such as *"Odalisque"* / *"Bathers"*, beachwear/bikini portraits, medical/anatomy illustrations, text-heavy document scans, fake system lock-screen dialogs, weapons/armor, animals in distress, corporate logos/mission insignia, multi-panel montages and figure plates such as *"Nectarine Fruit Development"* (categories `Montages of plants`, `3x2 arrays`), Wikimedia files with non-empty `Restrictions`, and `Attribution-NonCommercial` / `CC BY 1.0` licenses).
2. **No injected safety tags:** Source adapters must never inject synthetic safe tags (`"landscape"`, `"nature"`, `"featured"`, etc.) into `CandidateAsset.rawCategoriesAndTags`.
3. **Zero-escape rule:** No known-unsafe or hard-negative item may reach the approved state through L1–L4.
4. **Zero false-reject rule:** Known-safe nature, architecture, fine-art landscape, and NASA orbital/astronomy items must pass with zero false rejections.
5. **Fail-closed drills:** Automated tests force missing/uncertain safety classifications, non-HTTPS URLs, off-allowlist HTTP redirects, corrupt/ELF magic bytes, oversized image headers, and simulated decode failures, confirming nothing reaches the reserve.
6. **Re-evaluation test:** Changing `SafetyGate.COMPOSITE_GATE_VERSION` triggers re-evaluation of cached reserve items before apply and purges failures.
7. **Quarantine test:** Unapproved bytes are evaluated in memory/quarantine and never written to the approved wallpaper directory if any gate layer rejects.
8. **Blocklist persistence test:** A rejected image (matching SHA-256 or 64-bit `dHash`) persisted in SQLite `blocklist` survives repository recreation and is rejected at L2/L4 even when re-served by a different source.
9. **License & attribution test:** Fixtures for non-allowlisted licenses (`UNKNOWN_OR_RESTRICTED`, `BY-NC`, `CC BY 1.0`) and missing/`"Unknown author"` creator names on `CC BY` / `CC BY-SA` works are rejected at L1/L2, while verified Public Domain / CC0 works with institutional attribution pass.
